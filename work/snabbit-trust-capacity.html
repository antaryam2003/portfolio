Snabbit: what happens when ten minutes isn't enough

Snabbit has won the speed race — 15,000 Experts, 40,000 jobs a day, a $56M Series D. The 10-minute promise works often enough to fund all of it. So I didn't write a PRD for a missing feature. I wrote one for the six moments where the promise quietly breaks, and for what those breakdowns have in common: there is no policy engine behind the app.

02 — The problem

Six moments, each breaking a different part of the promise

Read across app-store reviews, press coverage and worker reporting, the failures are not random. They cluster at six recurring moments — and each damages a different piece of what Snabbit sells.

M1 Reliability
The clock stops being believable

An Expert assigned 30 minutes late on a 12-minute ETA, then an hour of no-show and an unanswered phone.

Cost: the ETA stops being something you can plan a day around — the exact pain the product exists to remove.
M2 Capacity
Packs are sold ahead of real capacity

Discounted prepaid packs bought, then no redeemable slots for weeks — while packs keep selling.

Cost: the customer has already paid for a promise local supply cannot keep.
M3 Recovery
Recovery is harder than the original problem

A 24-hour email SLA for something happening now; agents saying refund and reassignment are both impossible.

Cost: failures teach that fixing them is expensive. That lesson outlives the refund.
M4 Accountability
Misconduct has no enforced consequence

A Noida theft became local news; another reported case ended with the worker offered "a second chance."

Cost: "verified" becomes a claim about onboarding, not about what happens afterwards.
M5 Expert side
Worker safety is structurally unsolved

Experts fear harassment inside strangers' homes with no training — and invent their own coping routines.

Cost: the supply side absorbs a risk the platform created and does not yet manage.
M6 Expert side
Scheduling undermines the reliability being sold

A 1–3 PM break window overridden mid-shift, leaving some Experts without a meal until 4–5 PM.

Cost: an unpredictable workforce is a weak base for a ten-minute guarantee.
03 — The reframe

These are not support problems in disguise. They are the absence of a policy engine.

This is the sentence that reorganised the document. Every failure above has the same shape: a decision the system is not allowed to make, arriving as a human problem. Adding support headcount makes that queue faster. It does not make it smaller.

Symptom
"We cannot refund, we cannot allot new professional."
Missing engine
An agent-side authority rule. Nobody at the front line can approve what policy already allows.
Symptom
Booking locked because it is "past scheduled time."
Missing engine
An override path. A rule written for late customers fires hardest on the ones the platform failed.
Symptom
A theft report resolved with the worker's "second chance."
Missing engine
A defined suspension trigger. No evidence standard, no owner, no consequence.

05 — The proposal

Make the promise hold on both sides of the booking

Capacity sold matches capacity that can actually be delivered; a failing visit resolves itself before a human is needed; and Experts are protected proactively rather than left to invent their own coping routines.

01
Customers only buy what can be delivered

Packs and instant slots capped to forecast micro-market capacity — and any balance that can't be honoured refunds itself.

02
A failing booking fixes itself first

No-shows, lockouts and stuck refunds resolve against visit data, with a live human reachable when they can't.

03
Experts are protected before something goes wrong

Logged proactive check-ins, an enforced consequence for substantiated misconduct, and a break schedule the app honours.

07 — The Expert side

A trust PRD that only protects the buyer isn't a trust PRD

Snabbit's supply is 15,000 women entering strangers' homes on a ten-minute dispatch. Kavach, launched March 2026, is the company's own acknowledgment that reactive tooling wasn't enough. This PRD doesn't rebuild it — it makes its coverage verifiable.

Scheduling integrity
The break window is not the dispatcher's to take

Fixed for the shift once assigned, never silently overridden, adherence logged rather than surveyed — because an Expert who ate at 5 PM is not a reliable ten-minute promise.

Consent
Discomfort can be declared before acceptance

Without penalty to future assignment — and a recurring household can be permanently excluded. The informal screening Experts already do, made official.

Transparency
Earnings and leave stated in plain terms

Incentive structure and paid-vs-unpaid entitlement shown in-app, addressing reported confusion about time off in a business that sells "reduced absenteeism."

Journeys A, C and D — capacity honesty and the two recovery paths — hold 9 of the 18 P0s. That weighting is the argument: what must ship is not new service, it is the guarantee behind the service already sold.
Journey A · selected P0
  • Packs and instant slots sell only up to a micro-market's forecast redeemable capacity inside the validity window.
  • A booking past its scheduled time never auto-locks cancel, reschedule or refund.
  • Live status inconsistent with the promised arrival flags the booking at-risk and notifies the customer first.
Journeys C & D · selected P0
  • Structured issue reporting from the booking itself — no-show, incomplete task, item missing, misconduct.
  • Issues verifiable against visit data resolve automatically; refund or credit issued with no agent review.
  • Frontline agents can approve refunds, reassignment and cancellation inside published policy without escalating.
  • Substantiated theft or misconduct suspends the Expert pending review, with concrete support for a police report.
Journeys B & F · selected P0
  • Each Expert's break window is fixed for the shift once assigned and never silently reassigned.
  • Discreet periodic safety check-ins during every visit, logged for coverage verification.
  • Kavach check-ins logged with response rate and acknowledgment time, so coverage — not just existence — is auditable.
Deliberately out of scope

New service categories · pricing and unit economics · new-city expansion · payment methods · a formal insurance product for stolen or damaged items · rebuilding Kavach's risk model.

Where those intersect, the requirement is written as a constraint rather than a plan — and financial responsibility for verified theft is named as a business-policy decision this PRD does not get to make.

09 — How I'd know it worked

Four outcomes, each hard to game

Every one maps to a value prop, and every one is measured from system data rather than sentiment — because a support team can cut handle time without a single customer coming back.

↓
Unredeemed pack value

Share of purchased pack value that expires unused, per micro-market — and whatever remains must refund automatically, not on contact.

TESTS VALUE PROP 01 · CAPACITY HONESTY
↓
Failures needing a human

Share of no-show and lockout failures resolved by a person rather than by self-serve or automatic detection. Target: toward zero.

TESTS VALUE PROP 02 · SELF-HEALING RECOVERY
↑
Enforced consequence rate

Every substantiated misconduct or theft report ends in a tracked, enforced consequence — measured with time-to-suspension, not just count.

TESTS VALUE PROP 03 · ACCOUNTABILITY
↑
Logged break-window adherence

Measured from shift logs and correlated against Expert attrition and complaint rate. The one I'd defend the work on.

TESTS VALUE PROP 03 · SUPPLY-SIDE RELIABILITY
The number I'd ask for first
What share of support contacts are pack failures, no-shows, and misconduct reports?

That single split decides how each journey is sized and sequenced. Without it, this reads as one initiative — and twenty-seven requirements is a programme, not a sprint. It sits at the top of the PRD's open questions, next to who owns the decision to suspend an Expert today, and whether capping pack sales conflicts with the growth targets the last round was raised against.
